EUDR Customs Codes Explained: What Goes on the Declaration

On 30 December 2026 a customs computer in Rotterdam will check one field on the import declaration for a container of plywood. If the field is empty, the container does not move. This is what goes in it.

Adam Grant Grant Assurance
← Back to Insights

Key points

  • EU customs has created a set of document codes for EUDR. They exist in the tariff database now and become compulsory on 30 December 2026
  • Four codes cover almost every case: C716, C717, Y129 and Y141
  • The EU importer's customs agent lodges the declaration. The UK exporter's job is to get the right reference to the agent before the goods ship
  • The mapping of some exemption codes to tariff headings is still being finished, so check the current list before relying on it

Every import declaration in the EU carries a tariff number that says what the goods are, and may carry one or more document codes that say what paperwork stands behind them. A licence, a certificate of origin and a CITES permit each have a code. The EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) adds a new set, published by the Commission's customs directorate in September 2024 and revised on 23 September 2026 to match the amended regulation.

The codes sit in TARIC, the EU's integrated tariff, against every heading listed in Annex I of the regulation: logs, sawnwood, plywood, pulp, paper, wooden furniture and the rest. From 30 December 2026 customs will not release a consignment under one of those headings unless the declaration carries one of the codes. The customs condition is written in those terms: statement required, not presented, no exemption applies, import refused.

The four codes that matter

CodeWhat it saysWho uses it
C716A due diligence statement covers these goods. The declaration carries the statement's reference number from the EU Information SystemThe operator placing the goods on the EU market, or its customs agent. This is the normal case
C717A micro or small primary operator has filed its one-time simplified declaration, and the declaration carries that declaration's identifierMicro and small producers under the new Article 4a: a small forest owner or cooperative exporting its own output
Y129These goods are outside the regulation, although their tariff heading is listedAny declarant whose product falls under an "ex" heading in Annex I, where the heading is wider than the regulation's scope
Y141The declarant is a micro or small operator established as such by 31 December 2024, and the regulation does not yet apply to itSmall operators, for non-timber products only, until 29 June 2027

C716 is the one most UK exporters will meet. The EU importer, as the operator, submits a due diligence statement in the Information System and receives a reference number. Its customs agent enters C716 and that number on the import declaration. Without the number from the importer, the agent cannot complete the declaration, and without the data from the UK supplier, the importer cannot submit the statement. The chain runs backwards to the exporter's records. Our guide to EUDR for UK businesses lists what that data is.

Y129 is the one to understand. Several Annex I headings carry an "ex" prefix, which means only part of the heading is in scope. A declarant importing an out-of-scope product under one of those headings has to say so with Y129. Leave the field blank and customs treats the goods as in scope and stops them.

"Leave the field blank and customs treats the goods as in scope and stops them."

Y141 carries a date that the regulation states only indirectly. Micro and small operators established by 31 December 2024 have until 30 June 2027 for products other than those the EU Timber Regulation already covered. The customs code expires on 29 June 2027. For timber, paper and wooden furniture there is no such exemption, and a small operator uses C716 or C717 from 30 December 2026 like everyone else. Our EUDR deadlines article sets out every date.

The other codes

A further set covers narrower situations. C720 and C725 are for downstream operators re-importing goods that an earlier statement already covers, split by whether the operator is registered in the Information System. C718 covers goods first placed on the EU market between 30 June 2023 and 30 December 2026 and later exported or brought back. Y132 covers goods produced before 29 June 2023, Y133 recycled and second-hand goods, Y142 consumer-to-consumer parcels, Y187 samples and test material, Y190 packaging, and Y191 medicinal and marketing material. Y188 and Y196 are export-only codes for downstream operators. The full list, with the customs condition behind each, is in the Commission's TARIC working document.

What a UK exporter has to do

Nothing on the UK declaration. The EUDR codes exist in the EU tariff, and the UK's own Customs Declaration Service has no equivalent, so the export entry at Dover or Felixstowe is unchanged. The work is on the EU side, and the exporter's part of it comes before the goods ship.

Get the statement reference to your customer's customs agent with the shipping documents. If your customer is the operator, ask when its statement will be submitted and build that into the despatch date, because a container cannot be declared until the number exists. If your product falls under an "ex" heading and is outside scope, tell the agent in writing so that Y129 goes on the declaration. If you sell delivered duty paid and act as importer in the EU yourself, the statement and the code are your responsibility and your agent will need both from you.

Northern Ireland is a separate question. EUDR applies there directly, and how the EU customs conditions attach to goods moving from Great Britain is not addressed in the customs document.

Where this is still moving

The source is a working document from the Commission's customs directorate, not legislation. It states that the allocation of the packaging, sample and medicinal exemptions to individual tariff headings is still in progress, and it has been revised once already to follow the amended regulation. The codes and the 30 December date are fixed. The detail of which exemption applies to which heading may change before then, and a customs agent should work from the live TARIC entry rather than a printout.
