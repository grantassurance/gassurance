EUDR Deadlines: What Applies and When

The EU Deforestation Regulation applies on 30 December 2026 for large and medium companies, and on the same date for timber whatever the size of the business. The later date of 30 June 2027 covers less than most small firms assume.

Adam Grant Grant Assurance
← Back to Insights

Key points

  • 30 December 2026: large and medium operators and traders, all seven commodities
  • 30 December 2026: micro and small businesses too, for timber products that the EU Timber Regulation already covers
  • 30 June 2027: micro and small businesses, for everything else
  • 30 December 2027: products added to Annex I in September 2026, including soluble coffee

Two application dates appear in most summaries of the EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR): 30 December 2026 and 30 June 2027. A small timber business that plans for the second has the wrong date. Regulation (EU) 2025/2650 gave micro and small undertakings six more months, and it excluded from that extension the timber products already regulated under the EU Timber Regulation (EUTR).

Which size category applies

The regulation takes its size classes from the EU Accounting Directive, 2013/34/EU. An undertaking is small if it stays within two of three limits: a balance sheet total of €5 million, net turnover of €10 million and 50 employees. The micro limits are €450,000, €900,000 and 10 employees. Anything larger is medium or large and falls under the December 2026 date.

The extension is tied to status on 31 December 2024. A business that was small on that date qualifies. One that has shrunk since does not.

For a UK exporter the test applies to the EU operator, not to the exporter. A small Dorset joinery firm selling to a large Dutch distributor will be asked for data on the Dutch company's timetable.

Timber has one date

Sawnwood, plywood, flooring, joinery, pulp, paper and wooden furniture were all covered by the EUTR, so they fall under EUDR on 30 December 2026 for every operator. A micro business importing oak into France gets no extension.

Northern Ireland and Great Britain

EUDR applies in Northern Ireland on the EU dates. Goods moved from Great Britain to a Northern Ireland buyer from 30 December 2026 are supplied into an EUDR market, and Defra's guidance on those movements is still awaited.

Great Britain has no date yet. Defra confirmed on 23 June 2026 that it will consult on due diligence rules for businesses with turnover above £1 million, with legislation expected in 2027. The UK Timber Regulation continues unchanged until then. Our article on the UK deforestation rules covers the proposal.

Dates around EUDR

The Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition Directive has applied in EU member states since 27 September 2026. It restricts generic environmental claims and uncertified sustainability labels on consumer products, which includes claims about timber sourcing.

The Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation is further out. The Commission's working plan schedules product rules for furniture in 2028, and digital product passport obligations follow those rules after a transition period.

What to do with the time left

A first consignment trace takes about three months, which is roughly the time left before 30 December. Start with the priority lines. Rank products by sales into the EU and Northern Ireland, and ask suppliers of the top lines for plot geolocation and harvest dates now. Confirm each EU customer's size category in writing, because it sets the date that binds you. Our guide to EUDR for UK businesses lists what the customer will ask for.
